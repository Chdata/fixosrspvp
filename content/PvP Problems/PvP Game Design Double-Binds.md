
# Definition of "Double-Binds"

There's a number of **design traps** regarding PvP, or what I call “double-binds” or “triple-binds” or circular, self-defeating loops.

It's any situation where, in order to affect change, someone needs to give way, but no one is willing to.

Or situations where the designs or goals of things conflict with each other, or contradict each other, or are outright incompatible with each other.

These tend to be almost like oxymorons or paradoxes, because quite literally, some of these are impossible to resolve.

**It's kind of like this:**
- The following sentence is false.
- The previous sentence is true.

# Here’s a big list of examples of what I’m talking about.

**Dev time justification double-bind.** 
- PvP needs updates to be popular.
- However, PvP needs to be popular to get updates. Neither is happening.

If Jagex and the community ever wants PvP to move forward, we have to break out of this bind starting with one of these two options.

**Self-defeating PvP polls. Every population split is incompatible with supermajority polling.**
- A 50/50 PvMer and PvPer population cannot reach a 70% consensus.
- A 90/10 PvMer and PvPer population is PvMer biased.
- A 10/90 PvMer and PvPer population is PvPer biased.
- A 100% PvP population would likely still have factions of “types” of PvPers and still fail 70% polls.
- A 100% PvM population would fail all PvP updates.

And this is our present situation:
![[TheStateOfPvPPolls.png]]

[[The Polling System is Incompatible with PvP]]

If we ever want to break out of this impossible scenario, the only way is to change the relationship between how PvP is developed and the polling system.


**Changing risk vs reward to either lower barriers to entry or make it more worth doing is a triple-bind.**
- PKers don't want to lower risk, so they want to increase reward.
- The obvious way is to increase gold/h. However, Jagex does not want to increase gold generation in the economy, so the only way left is unique items. 
- However, PvMers don't want unique game progression to come from the Wilderness, so all means of increasing reward are (seemingly) locked out. 
- So the only other way is to decrease risk. However, return to the first bullet point. 

**For mains: Should the PvMer be more viable, or should the PKer be more viable?**
- Main PvMers don't go to the Wilderness because they'll just lose money. 
- Player killers make money by killing other players. 
- But that just means that other players lose money. The money has to come from somewhere.
- If Mains got good enough to survive most of the time, PKers would stop profiting. Then they would die out or complain that PvMers survive too much, advocating for them to die more. 
- So Mains must remain killable, but then that makes them lose money, so they die out engaging with the Wilderness or they ask to be more survivable.
- Mains must remain killable, but the fact that most people struggle too much, or that people don't perceive they can win, keeps them out.

**Risk vs Power Viability Double-Bind**
- You need to risk more to be powerful, but you don't want to risk more because it's expensive.
- So you want to risk less, but it makes you less powerful, so you lose more often, losing money more often.

**Cat & Mouse game balance double-bind**
- PvMers must be reasonably able to survive.
- But PKers must be reasonably able to confirm kills.

**Old vs New double-bind**
- Players want to stick to traditional, nostalgic designs. 
- However, those very designs are limiting factors that turn off new players from trying PvP. So we want modern, and novel designs that change what is traditional or “oldschool”.

**Casual vs Competitive double-bind**
- Veteran players want to retain old, high skill ceiling designs.
- However, those very designs are limiting factors that make it difficult for new players to even try PvP, who are put off by those designs.

**High requirements vs low requirements. Stopping bots vs limiting legitimate players.**
- Activities having low requirements makes them subject to more bots flooding the activity, so one idea is to just put higher requirements on things.

- However, putting higher requirements on things further alienates real players too, making the activities less populated.
	Hence certain players being left out for example, when visiting the Wilderness bosses with friends.
	Or players feeling bad about the 1 win requirement for Last Man Standing, seeing it as an insurmountable goal.


**Motivation to learn before having fun**
- PvP right now feels like players need to learn up to a certain skill floor before they can have fun.
- But in reality, PvP needs to be fun first in order for players to go through the effort of learning.

**Punishing vs Forgiving**
- The Wilderness must be punishing on death because it’s a risky area.
- However, PvP must be more forgiving if it’s going to appeal to a wider, more casual audience.

**Risk PvP is very niche.**
- PKers want risk PKing to be super popular and not niche.
- However, risk PKing itself is a niche of a niche. The more risky something is, the less players do it. The less risky it is, the more players do it.

**Where does new blood come from?**
10% of the population are already PvPers, and 90% are PvMers, so where does new blood come from if PKers don’t want to appeal to PvMers?

Source: [[OSRS Population Stats]]

**Balance Change Polling Double-Bind:**
- Balance Changes need to be unpolled.
- New Armour or Weapon Rewards are Balance Changes.
- Rewards need to be polled.

**Wilderness Risk vs Power Triple-Bind where there isn't really a viable option for gameplay.**
1. Either you bring absolutely nothing and have 0 risk, but cannot possibly fight back, or
2. You bring a minimal risk 50k loadout, and now you have extremely weak equipment so you are very likely to die most of the time anyway, or
3. You bring an actually decent loadout, and now you're risking 300k or 40m depending on what that loadout is, and you are losing a lot more gold on death.

All 3 options suck for new players, casual players, and risk-averse players.
[[The Majority of the Playerbase is Risk-Averse]]

It's not a problem for PKers because they are both willing to lose huge amounts, [[PKers are desensitized to risk]], and they already have several years of skill and experience built up that they can reliably kill other players to earn back their losses.

# How do we break out of these circular problems?

The way out of any one of these loops is to break the loop, at the sacrifice of one link in the chain. 

This can only be done by first acknowledging, realizing, and naming the loop. 

Then, there is usually some kind of highly specific way out of them that does kind of work for everyone, or works for the largest group possible, but it's really very very specific or something out-of-the-box.

Normally, it's the developers who break us out of loops because they design the game, and players adapt.

**All of this happens because the polling system prioritizes the preferences of every single opposing community faction.** 
- So the way out would be to not poll PvP changes. 
- But Jagex does not want to move away from polling.
- Wait, that pattern sounds familiar.


# How are we currently solving each of these "double-binds"?

Every time there is a stalemate, we give up and choose the secret polling option D: “Do nothing”. 

Or we end up with a half-finished broken solution like the PvP Arena or adding more activities and rewards to the Wilderness without really looking at the barriers to entry of the Wilderness.

But the majority of the time, we just do nothing because we are double or triple-binded by the fact that there are just permanently opposed factions in regards to PvP content.

Everyone wants to die on their hill about how PvP should be, so it seems to be impossible to find a way forward that satisfies everyone.

**Old vs New double-bind**

My answer to “old vs new” is that the old shit doesn’t work. We have no choice but to try new things and do our best to make it honor the old systems but not be blinded by nostalgia.


**Casual vs Competitive double-bind**

Casual vs competitive is a debate that happens in every single multiplayer PvP game that has a competitive scene or that has "high skill players", which is pretty much every game in existence.

Generally, what’s good for competitive is not great for casual, and what’s great for casual is not good for competitive.

The problem in Oldschool Runescape is that most PvP does not appeal to a casual playerbase.

The answer is that it needs to be reworked in ways that make it appeal better to a casual playerbase.

Again, this is what pretty much every solution I have is focused around, but I do try not to disrupt competitive or high skill aspects with most of my ideas because we want to preserve as much of that as we can.


**High requirements vs low requirements. Stopping bots vs limiting legitimate players.**

My solution here is to have high requirements for obtaining monetary rewards, but low requirements for participating in activities.

Additionally, the more untradeable rewards, the better, because those are pointless for bots.

This is something I go into more detail on in the Discord and in the relevant suggestions.

**Motivation to learn before having fun**

PvP needs to be fun almost regardless of what your current skill level is.

You should be able to have fun even if you’re hilariously bad and clueless at PvP.

For example, I still enjoyed Castle Wars and Soul Wars despite being a low level noob.

This ties into making the game better for casual players. Whatever makes it better for casual players will make it more fun to learn.

We should not be expecting players to have to learn PvP on a very deep level before they can "start" having fun.

Generally speaking, PvP is fun even if you suck at it, as long as you're up against people who are on your level.

It's not fun when you're new and suddenly you have to fight someone who moves like Odablock.

Player's skill levels in games also tends to plateau at a certain point for years. It's perfectly possible for a player to be unable to even reach higher skill levels.

That represents a problem if they are then not able to enjoy the game at their own skill level.

We should still expect some kind of skill floor, like understanding item and prayer switching, and using a minimum level of strength of armor and weapons relative to your opponents.

But the skill floor should not involve understanding PID, 1-tick combos, 8-way tribridding, DD or Freeze + Step-Under shenanigans, or freeze log.

At most the skill floor should be "knowing how to use a special attack", some basic Venge PKing, some basic lazy bridding, and some basic escape gameplay.

Both of these solutions are offering a "low skill" way to engage with the Wilderness for both PvPers and PvMers.

**Where does new blood come from?**

![[JagexGlobalImage.png]]

If 10% of players are PvPers (already do PvP) and 90% are PvMers (or some other kind of non-PvPer), but PKers don’t want to appeal to PvMers, then how do we get new blood?

[[OSRS Population Stats]]

Well, we do appeal to PvMers. Not every PvMers is strictly a “no PvP” gamer. Many might very well be “fence-sitters” who only don’t PvP because it’s poorly designed everywhere.

We can and should completely ignore players who strictly don't want to engage in PvP encounters.

PvP should not appeal to players who do not want to engage in PvP encounters.

We can also appeal to players who are not part of the current population, which means new players, and retired veterans, and PKers who have quit.

**Summary:**
- Existing PKers (10% of our population) already engage with PvP.
- They don't need to be convinced to engage with it.

**Therefore, new blood comes from these sources:**
1. Existing Mains who don't currently PvP (60% of our population)
2. Existing Ironmen (30% of our population)
3. New players. (Immeasurable... but Mod North says that over 50% of OSRS players started within the last couple of years).
4. Returning retired veterans. (Immeasurable).
5. Returning PKers who have quit. (Immeasurable... but Runescape Private Server playercounts might give you some kind of ballpark number).

**No, I am not expecting 100% conversion.**

We don't need all 60% of our Mains and all 30% of our Ironmen.

Again, it's perfectly reasonable that plenty of players are not interested in PvP whatsoever. This is just normal in all of gaming.

Even if we got half of that, 30% Mains and 15% Mains, that's an significant improvement from just 10% of players to 55% of players.

Even if we got one TENTH of that, a whopping 6% + 3%, that would actually nearly double the PvP playerbase from 10% to 19%.

And that's not counting potential traffic from new or returning players.
